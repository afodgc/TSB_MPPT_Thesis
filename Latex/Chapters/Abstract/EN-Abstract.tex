\noindent Solar-powered vehicles rely on solar energy to charge their batteries and maximize their range, particularly solar-powered boats need an effective energy conversion in rapidly changing environmental conditions. In this context, the \ac{mppt} charger plays a critical role in energy conversion, by ensuring that the solar panels operate on their \ac{mpp} while charging a battery. This work focus on the study, design, simulation, development and test of a fast response \ac{mppt} charger for \ac{tsb}.

This thesis present the design and implementation of a \ac{mppt} charger with fast response capable of being used in \ac{sg01}. The prototype was designed to charge a lithium-ion battery of 43.2~V nominal voltage, converting the energy provided by the solar panel with high efficiency. The proposed charger is based on a synchronous boost converter operating at 100 kHz. 
% The power stage, sensing circuits, processing unit, and communication interfaces were designed according to the requirements of the application. 
An STM32 \ac{mcu} was used to implement the control firmware, including the \ac{mppt} algorithm, battery charging state machine, protections, and CAN and serial communication.

Different \ac{mppt} techniques and DC-DC converter topologies were analyzed to identify a suitable solution for the dynamic operating conditions of the vessel. Particular attention was given to the trade-off between tracking speed and accuracy, leading to the implementation of a \ac{peo} algorithm. The converter and \ac{pv} system were simulated in both LTspice and Matlab to validate the component selection and algorithm parameters. A custom \ac{pcb} was designed, manufactured and solder by hand. 
% Experimental results were obtained to optimize and validate the functionality of the prototype.

The experimental results demonstrate success of the proposed architecture and show that the implemented \ac{mppt} algorithm can respond rapidly to changes in irradiance, with the measured tracking response being suitable for the environmental variations identified for \ac{sg01}. The devolved prototype also provides a practical method to configure the device using a computer application which can also be used for data analysis. The resulting system provides a dedicated, configurable, and low-cost \ac{mppt} charger for \ac{tsb}, while establishing a platform for further improvements in efficiency, tracking performance, and integration into future vessels.
